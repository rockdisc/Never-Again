Measured in radians per second or rpm.

The angular acceleration is the same as regular [[Acceleration]]:

$$
\alpha = \frac{\delta \omega}{\delta t}
$$
Rotational kinematics takes direct equations from linear [[Kinematics]], though with rotational values, this means that the equations will translate over. 
$$
\begin{align}
\omega _{f}= \omega_{i} +  \alpha t  \\
\Delta \theta=\omega_{i}t+\frac{1}{2} \alpha t^2 \\
\omega^2_{f}=\omega ^2_{i}+2\alpha \Delta \theta
\end{align}

$$

This also goes into dynamics with things like $F=ma$ turning into:
$$
\sum\tau=I \alpha
$$
$\tau$ is [[Torque]]. I is rotational inertia. 
## Rotational Inertia 
The smaller it is, the faster it rolls. It can be represented as: 
$$
I=\sum mr^2
$$
for a point mass, though there are several different for different shapes. These can be derived by looking at the shape and seeing the relationship between the edges to the [[Center of Mass]].

### Parallel Axis Theorem 
$$
I_{x}=I_{cm}+m\Delta x^2
$$
This shows that the least the inertia will be is when the axis of rotation is at the center of mass.
