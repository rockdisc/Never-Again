You walk into an art museum, Renaissance paintings, statues depicting humans with perfect anatomical accuracy, and finally, when you get near the end, the biggest and most important exhibit the museum wants you to remember, a giant canvas the size of a wall filled with red, yellow, and blue. What is that?  PICTURE
There are a lot of ways to describe modern art; to the average person, a few come to mind. Useless, meaningless, an excuse for money-laundering, or even a disgrace on the history of art. But is that really the case? Why do enthusiasts like them so much?
To explain these questions lets first FRAMING DEVICE

Walk through the museum, what is a good piece of art, or one that should be in the museum; then when we come to a painting, let's look at the placard, learn how to look at a painting; and finally lets look at the art piece, or several, and understand them. 

Let's look at these 2 paintings:

Lucifer by Jackson Pollock and Christmas Service by Thomas Kinkade, which of these would you think was considered a masterpiece? Context will tell you it is most likely Lucifer. 
From the Stanford Collection, Lucifer is 8 feet long, Pollack had to paint it with it on the ground. And the way he painted it was special as well, he would dip is brush in the paint bucket and threw the paint on there when in a drug-influenced trance. Pollack said in a 1951 radio interview that older styles of painting could not express the chaos of this age, with nukes and radios, so a new style was needed. It sold for 31 million dollars. 
This is Christmas Service by Kinkade, from his own website. Painted by Kinkade Studios, hence the artist's name is unknown, the original is not sold, but prints of it are, for $200s. It is a painting of a small village in the mountains, and a church, that is it. It is in its very bones a painting made to make money. 
A Guardian article from 2006, the prime of Kinkade, shows that he had an empire built on his consumable art. He believes art is relevant if people buy it, not if it comments on society, to him art is not a way of expressing yourself and good art is not something that is something that is bigger than itself, he believes that the message of art is not a combination of the result, the artist, and the process, but just the result. The LA times interviewed artist shows their hate of this rhetoric, to them, and people who curate museums, art that should be in museums and costly is art that has a message beyond the art itself, seems obscure, but that is art itself, it can be anything, as long as it says something, museum level art is something that says something beyond itself. 

And throughout history this was the case. It was always realism that was on top, but according to the Kiama Gallery, around the 1860s art started experimenting with other art styles, like Monet and Impression, Sunrise, he painted the impression he got from the sunrise, not trying to be realist, but using the brush strokes to express the feeling of it, art to show that art does not have to imitate reality, but can stand on its own. Even back in the day there was a lot of criticism against this, the same type we see today, look at pointalism, made of dots, what type of joke is this, it does not even look like art, so they would say. But this conception of art has changed, the way we look at art has changed, so how exactly do we look at art?

This is untitled from Basquiat, just by looking at it, we see a skull and what looks like scribbles, but as told in Marshall Gallery, we have to look at the story around the art, look at the placard. From the Brooklyn Museum, Basquiat was a black artist in New York, the use of damaged skulls shows the effects on the black body, the crown on his head crossed out with people playing tic tac to, a childrens game. Words marked out, not being able to be educated. The harshness in colors and shapes show the harshness in life. Just knowing a little bit of information about the artist can shape the entire painting. 
But this does not mean that all art is like this, there is a lot of money laundering in the art world. An art investigatory journal called Art and Object wrote about the amount that happens, and that it is not modern art for the most part, but instead all famous art, this painting, Hannibal by Basqiuat was a victim to it, as it is now lost. Paintings with meaning are the ones that are laundered the most. So lets look at these paintings with these meanings, up as that painting and placard as well. 

This is Levitating Mass, from the LA Museum of Art, First just looking at the piece, it a rock, and you are directed to walk under it, and it looks like only these small supports are stopping it from falling on you. Now lets look at the placard, again from the Museum, from the start of humanity humans made art on these type of rock, now it is art again, but to make it art in this way, they had to transport it 105 miles and precariously position it here for it to be safe, a marvel of human ingenuity. The art should invoke a feeling of danger, though completely safe, many people choose not to walk under the thousands of pounds above them, this also shows the weight of history put on artists, there are all of these beautiful art in the past, art is really the only field where things from that long ago are compared to modern creations. And that puts this weight on artists. See how what looked like a random rock before now looks like art. 

Let's go back to the start, the first painting, its name, Who’s Afraid of Red, Yellow, and Blue iii, a small stripe of blue, and even smaller of Yellow. From the Stedelijk Museum, 17 feet long, and not a single brush stroke can be found. The placard tells you that it is supposed to show the place of humans, the artist, Newman, wanted you to walk close to it so that your whole field of view, and be consumed. Now why did I pick this painting, and not the others in this collection? Well, something happened to this painting, it was torn, someone was afraid of red yellow and blue. Aside from being a commentary on human’s place in the world, it is also talking about the idea of doing abstract art, especially in times where it is looked down upon. And this shows how much they hated it. An art revivalist tried to fix it, but the red was not as vivid and the smallest evidence of brush strokes were found. Newman would use things like eggshells in his paints for them to be this vivid and the revival attempt showed the value of the original painting. 

Now that we are done with these artworks, this process of walking up to a piece, then looking at the placard, and finally looking at and understanding it can be applied to all other pieces of art. This is something that can be done before judging that piece of art. Thank you.
